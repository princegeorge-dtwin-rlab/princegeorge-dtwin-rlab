Why I Am Building Digital Twins from First Principles

Starting a transparent engineering journey: defining the questions, the methods, and the evidence I will need before I can claim that a model works.

22 November 2026 · Research direction · Programme initiated — results pending
← Back to research journal

My work in chemical and environmental engineering has repeatedly brought me back to one question: how can we make better decisions about a physical process when measurements are limited, conditions are changing, and the original design assumptions no longer fully describe the plant?

Digital Twin technology offers a promising direction, but I do not want to begin with an attractive dashboard and then search for a model to justify it. I want to begin with the process itself—its physics, chemistry, uncertainty and operational constraints.

What I bring to this investigation

My background includes a Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering and professional experience in process engineering, water and wastewater treatment, computational modelling, research and teaching. These areas provide the engineering foundation for the programme, but a functioning digital twin requires additional competence in real-time data integration, software engineering, model calibration and operational validation.

The questions I want to answer

  • What level of model complexity is justified by the available measurements?
  • How can first-principles models and data-driven methods complement each other without concealing model limitations?
  • How can a system distinguish physical process faults from faulty sensors or inaccurate assumptions?
  • What validation evidence is necessary before giving operational recommendations?
  • Can a reusable digital twin architecture work across mechanical, chemical, biological and electrochemical systems?
My working principle

A simulation is not automatically a digital twin. I will label synthetic data as synthetic, report unsuccessful tests, and avoid claiming that a prototype or model is industrially validated until there is suitable evidence.

The project-based research programme

I am organizing the work into twenty-four focused minor projects and eight integrated capstone applications. The initial focus is on engineering modelling and connectivity; later work will address biological systems, IAQ and HVAC, electrochemical devices, and anaerobic digestion.

The first experiment: a centrifugal pump and piping network

My first minor project will investigate the intersection of a pump characteristic curve with a system head curve. It is deliberately modest: before connecting real-time data or predictive algorithms, I need to demonstrate that the calculated operating point is physically meaningful.

  1. State the operating conditions and units; identify missing input information.
  2. Derive the pump and system relationships and solve for the operating point.
  3. Implement the equations in Python and check the numerical solution independently.
  4. Apply sensitivity tests for changing pipe resistance and operating conditions.
  5. Record mistakes, questionable assumptions, corrected calculations and remaining uncertainty.
  6. Connect the verified model to simulated telemetry before considering real sensors.

No pump simulation results are presented in this first entry. They belong in the next journal article after the calculations and tests have actually been performed.

How I will document failures

For each unexpected result, I intend to record the observed symptom, the initial explanation, competing hypotheses, the tests performed, and what the evidence supports. A converged numerical solution is not necessarily a correct engineering solution. Equally, a model that fails may reveal a mistaken boundary condition, an unsuitable assumption or an inadequately observed process.

What readers should expect

This journal will combine engineering derivations, code, experimental planning, validation plots, design decisions and critical reviews of emerging technologies. Completed results will be separated from planned work. Proprietary plant data, client information and potentially protectable inventions will not be published without appropriate permission and review.
