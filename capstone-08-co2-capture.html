Capstone research · 08 · Two parallel technologies

Flue-Gas CO₂ Capture Digital Twin:
Absorption + Adsorption

Two equally important modelling pathways—liquid-solvent absorption with regeneration, and solid-sorbent adsorption with cyclic regeneration—integrated in one comparable industrial Digital Twin framework.

Planned research · No validation claimed

← Back to the capstone portfolio

The research question

For a defined industrial flue-gas stream, how do absorption and adsorption compare in CO₂ recovery, product purity, energy demand, resilience to disturbances, maintainability and cost?

The two capture technologies will be developed as parallel, independently validated process models and then brought together in a comparative, data-connected Digital Twin. Neither technology is assumed to be universally superior. A representative steel-industry flue-gas case is a proposed starting point, contingent on a defensible gas analysis and impurity-control basis.

Research status: This is a planned capstone, not a report of completed simulations or experimental findings. Benchmark values and results will be published only after their sources, assumptions, and validation methods are documented.

Two process routes, one consistent feed basis

Industrial flue gas + characterization + route-specific pretreatment
↓
Route A · AbsorptionConditioning → gas–liquid absorber → rich solvent → regeneration / stripper → lean-solvent recycleOutputs: recovered CO₂, solvent loading, steam duty, pumping, emissions and degradation indicators
Route B · AdsorptionConditioning → packed adsorption bed(s) → breakthrough → PSA / TSA / TVSA regeneration → cyclic operationOutputs: recovered CO₂, working capacity, bed productivity, cycle energy, pressure drop and sorbent condition
Common reporting boundary → CO₂ recovery and purity · Heat and electricity · Water and material use · Operability · Indicative cost

PSA = pressure-swing adsorption; TSA = temperature-swing adsorption; TVSA = temperature-vacuum-swing adsorption. Cycles will be shortlisted after feed partial pressure, temperature and water content are characterized.

The two technical modelling workstreams

Route A · Gas–liquid

Absorption + solvent regeneration

Use aqueous amine absorption as the reference case, with appropriate thermodynamics and rate-based treatment where justified.

  • Gas–liquid equilibrium, reaction kinetics and interfacial mass transfer
  • Absorber hydraulics, temperature bulge and pressure drop
  • Lean/rich loading, circulation, stripper and reboiler energy
  • Heat integration, water balance, solvent losses and aging indicators
  • Dynamic responses to flow, concentration and utility disturbances
Route B · Gas–solid

Adsorption + cyclic regeneration

Compare evidence-supported reference sorbents (such as zeolites, activated carbon, amine-functionalized porous supports or selected MOFs), accounting for humidity, stability and practical cyclic working capacity.

  • CO₂ adsorption isotherms (e.g., Langmuir/Toth where suitable) and humidity competition
  • Axial packed-bed balances, mass-transfer kinetics and breakthrough curves
  • Heat of adsorption, thermal fronts and bed pressure drop
  • PSA/TSA/TVSA cycle design, working capacity and cycle productivity
  • Sorbent regeneration, attrition/deactivation and feed-contaminant effects

Three supporting minor projects

M22 · Shared basis

Flue-gas characterization

Build component and energy balances; evaluate CO₂ partial pressure, flow disturbances, SOₓ/NOₓ, oxygen, particulates and moisture. Define comparable system boundaries and feed cases.

M23 · Absorption

Gas–liquid capture model

Calculate absorber dynamics, solvent circulation, lean/rich loading, desorption and regeneration heat duty. Verify mass and heat balances.

M24 · Adsorption

Gas–solid capture model

Calculate equilibrium and rate effects, breakthrough time, cyclic regeneration, pressure drop, specific energy and sorbent performance.

The capstone integration phase will compare M23 and M24 against the M22 shared basis and add state estimation, hybrid modelling, uncertainty analysis, diagnostics and a unified dashboard.

From process models to a Digital Twin

  1. Define the industrial case. Fix a source-specific gas composition, variability envelope, reference capture target and CO₂ product requirement.
  2. Verify the physics separately. Validate mass and energy balances; fit solvent-equilibrium/kinetic data and sorbent equilibrium/kinetic data only against documented sources or measured data.
  3. Simulate off-design behaviour. Test gas-flow, CO₂ fraction, temperature, humidity, impurity and utility disturbances for each route.
  4. Build dynamic predictive models. Produce a physics-based model and, if data permit, an ML-assisted or reduced-order version for each technology.
  5. Connect data and track uncertainty. Incorporate time-stamped sensor or pilot data when available, quantify prediction errors and flag out-of-distribution operation.
  6. Show the engineering choice. Present comparable recovery, energy, purity and operating constraints on one dashboard. The output is advisory until plant-specific validation and safety review.

Proposed computational tools

Aspen Plus or suitable process simulation for the solvent circuit, Python/SciPy for absorber/adsorber dynamics and cyclic scheduling, COMSOL for selected coupled transport studies, and time-series telemetry/dashboard tools if prototype or pilot measurements become available.

What a client or interview panel will see

Demonstration A

Live absorption flowsheet with flue-gas disturbances, lean/rich loading, CO₂ removal, regeneration duty and fault scenarios.

Demonstration B

Animated adsorption breakthrough, dynamic bed temperature and pressure, PSA/TSA/TVSA cycle state and predicted CO₂ product flow.

Cross-technology decision dashboard

Same reference feed and target; side-by-side metrics, operating envelopes, uncertainty ranges and an evidence-backed technology selection narrative.

Research journal

Model assumptions, unsuccessful approaches, parameter sources, verification tests, discrepancies and next experiments.

Technical risks to acknowledge

  • Flue-gas impurities and humidity affect the two processes differently; a common inlet feed does not imply identical pretreatment.
  • Amine absorption is strongly dependent on correct thermodynamic and rate-based assumptions; equilibrium-only models can misestimate column size/performance.
  • Adsorption capacity from a single isotherm is not the same as practical cyclic working capacity; transient heat and mass transfer and regeneration losses matter.
  • Material ageing, fouling, product purification and CO₂ compression requirements must not be omitted from cost and energy comparisons.
  • Validation should use independent experiments, published benchmark cases or measured pilot observations, clearly labelled by evidence level.
