Engineering research question

Can a hybrid, data-connected model predict treated-water quality, filter or membrane fouling, recovery and energy demand before a plant moves outside its operating envelope?

Select a realistic raw-water quality and required treated-water specification. Treatment units must be justified by those requirements; coagulation, filtration, UF and RO are alternatives or successive stages only where appropriate.

Evidence status: This page outlines a proposed capstone. It does not claim that measurements, model calibration, validation, or industrial deployment have already occurred.

Proposed process boundaries

Raw-water characterization → equalization / pretreatment → optional coagulation–flocculation–clarification → granular filtration → optional UF → optional RO / polishing → treated-water tank, with backwash, reject and sludge streams explicitly accounted for.

Key physical and computational models

  • Transient hydraulic and contaminant mass balances; variable raw-water turbidity and iron/manganese where relevant
  • Coagulant demand and clarification performance correlations grounded in jar tests
  • Media-filter pressure loss and backwash operation; membrane flux and transmembrane pressure trends
  • RO water and salt balances, permeate conductivity, recovery, specific energy and concentrate management
  • Soft sensors, fault detection and an operator advisory layer with clear model uncertainty

Supporting minor projects

M19 — Filtration and fouling dynamics

Model media-filter head loss and breakthrough, with evidence-based backwash triggers; consider pretreatment effects on loading.

M20 — UF/RO membrane performance

Model UF flux decline and RO water/salt balances, recovery, conductivity, fouling and specific energy.

M21 — Water-quality and energy optimization

Integrate sensor streams and optimize product quality, recovery, energy, chemical consumption and cleaning constraints.

Validation and integration milestones

  1. Complete water balance for every treatment and reject stream
  2. Calibrate correlations against jar-test and/or plant measurements; identify assumptions when measured data are unavailable
  3. Test high-turbidity events, membrane fouling, backwash and conductivity disturbances
  4. Compare predicted quality and energy against held-out measurements or designated synthetic test scenarios
  5. Build a dashboard with alarm thresholds and traceable operator recommendations

Proposed software / data architecture

Python (SciPy / pandas), DWSIM where appropriate, membrane data or spreadsheet models, MQTT, time-series database, Streamlit or Grafana. Start with simulated data; upgrade to monitored pilot/site data only when authorized.

What an interview panel or client could see

An interactive plant schematic showing feed-water quality, selected treatment route, predicted product quality, filter / membrane condition, chemical consumption and energy intensity during changing raw-water conditions.

Limitations and research risks to document

  • Water quality cannot be reliably predicted from turbidity alone
  • Coagulation removal correlations depend on source water and jar-test conditions
  • Membrane fouling models require calibration; RO modelling needs feed-ion chemistry and membrane details
  • Regulatory suitability must be confirmed through actual sampling and accredited testing
